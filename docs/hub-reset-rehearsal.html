Two constraints that actually bite

The setup portal closes after 10 minutes and the Hub restarts. That is plenty — unless you spend it hunting for the Wi-Fi password. Have it before you press anything.

The ESP32 is 2.4 GHz only. If the venue offers a 5 GHz network, or a single name that hides both, the Hub may not see it at all. Worth asking the venue before the day.

The run

  1. Note what “working” looks like now

    Open the app or wattwise.site and confirm readings are arriving. You need a before state, or you cannot tell that the after state is real.

    Readings update every 1.5–5 s while the Hub is online. Analytics shows a live figure rather than "No signal".

  2. Connect serial — optional, but it turns guesswork into reading

    USB to the laptop, any serial monitor at 115200. Type status and press Enter.

    [STATUS] FW=relay-cloud-dualpzem-1.5.1 WiFi=connected Relay1=OFF Relay2=OFF TelemetryMode=PZEM

    Without serial this still works — you just judge each step by the Wi-Fi list and the app instead of by the messages.

  3. Hold the BOOT button for 5 seconds

    Powered on, hold it down and keep holding. Five seconds, not a tap — BOOT is also the flash pin, so a short press is deliberately harmless.

    [PROV] BOOT held - keep holding to clear Wi-Fi settings. ← at 1 s [PROV] Factory reset: clearing Wi-Fi and restarting. ← at 5 s

    Nothing after 8–10 seconds? The Hub may be running firmware older than the reset button. Re-flash from the repo and start again.

  4. Join the Hub's own network

    On your phone or laptop, look for a network named WattWise‑Hub‑XXXX. The last four characters come from the Hub's MAC address, so you will not know them until it appears. Join it with the setup password above.

    Give it about 10 seconds after the restart before you expect to see it.

  5. Open the setup page

    Most phones pop it up on their own. If not, go to http://192.168.4.1 — any address you type gets redirected there anyway.

    A list of nearby networks, with a Rescan button if the one you want is missing.

  6. Pick the network, enter its password, save

    The Hub saves and restarts itself. Your phone drops off the Hub's network at that moment — that is the restart, not a failure.

    [WiFi] Connecting to KIANO-4G ......... [WiFi] Connected. IP: 192.168.x.x

    Typed the password wrong? The Hub notices it has never connected with these details, wipes them, and reopens the portal by itself. Just go back to step 4. Credentials that have worked before are kept instead, so a router being off never forces you through setup again.

  7. Confirm it is reporting — in the app, not the portal

    The portal saying “saved” only means it stored the password. The real signal is fresh readings arriving. Open the app and watch the outlet cards.

    Live watts updating, and Settings showing the Hub as online.

    Joined the network but nothing arrives? Check Settings → Hub status. If it says the device is not linked, re-pair by scanning the QR code.

  8. Pull the power, put it back

    The step nobody runs, and the one that matches what happens if the venue's power flickers. Unplug the Hub, wait a few seconds, plug it back in.

    It rejoins without the setup portal, and both relays come back OFF rather than in a remembered state.

    If it opens the portal again instead, the credentials were never marked as verified — meaning step 6 did not actually complete. Run it again.

If it goes sideways

What you seeWhat it means, and what to do
Holding BOOT does nothingFirmware predates the reset button. Re-flash from the repo, then start again.
No WattWise-Hub-… networkWait 10 s after the restart. Check you are looking at the 2.4 GHz list — some phones hide it.
Setup page will not loadConfirm you are joined to the Hub's network, not your usual one. Type http://192.168.4.1 directly.
Network list is emptyTap Rescan. The Hub stops its own connection attempts before scanning, so the list fills on the second try.
Portal closed on its ownThe 10 minutes elapsed and it restarted. Hold BOOT for 5 s and go again.
Connected, but no readingsWi-Fi is fine; pairing is not. Settings → Hub status, then re-scan the QR code.

Serial commands, while you are in there

help | status | telemetry | relay1 on|off | relay2 on|off | sim on|off
One of those is not for the defence

sim on makes the Hub publish invented readings instead of what the sensors measure. It exists for testing the cloud path without a live load. Running it during an evaluation would put fabricated numbers in front of a panel. Leave it off, and check status reads TelemetryMode=PZEM before any demo.

Why this is worth 15 minutes

Everything else in WattWise has been exercised on real hardware — the cutoff has tripped, timers have fired, pairing has been done more than once. This has not. The Hub has only ever been on one network.

The mock defence is on someone else's Wi-Fi. If the reset-and-reconnect path has a problem, the choice is finding out here, at a desk, with the repo open — or finding out in the room with a panel waiting. That is the whole argument.
