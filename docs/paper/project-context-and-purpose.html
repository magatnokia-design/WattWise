Project Context

Chapter I › replaces the line “Introduce your locale and explain why you chose it”

The template asks two things here: where, and why there. The second is the one panels press on, because “it is where we live” is a reason to pick a site but not a reason the study needs that site. The answer below is that the tenancy itself is the constraint — an occupant who cannot modify the distribution panel can only be served by a device that sits between the socket and the appliance. That is why WattWise is a plug-in unit controlling two outlets, and saying so turns your locale from a convenience into a justification.

Paste — Chapter I › Project Context

WattWise was developed for households occupying rented apartment and boarding rooms in [BARANGAY], Sto. Tomas, Pampanga, within the franchise area of the Pampanga Electric Cooperative III (PELCO III). [NUMBER] such units were identified in the immediate locale, of which thirty-two met the criteria used in this study: occupants who use low-power plug-in appliances and who pay, or contribute to, the household’s monthly electricity bill.

This locale was selected because it exhibits the problem the study addresses in its sharpest form. A rented room is metered as a single point. Where the landlord sub-meters, the occupant receives one figure and an amount due; where the unit is billed directly, the statement still reports only a total for the whole room. In neither case can the occupant attribute that total to any particular appliance. The tenant is therefore asked to reduce a cost whose composition is not visible to them, and the only instrument available — the monthly bill — arrives after every decision that produced it has already been made.

The tenancy also constrains what any solution may do. An occupant cannot modify the distribution panel, install a whole-house meter, or alter fixed wiring, since the electrical installation belongs to the landlord and any intervention would require consent and, in most cases, a licensed electrician. A measuring device intended for this locale must therefore sit between the existing outlet and the appliance, be installed and removed without tools, and leave the premises unaltered. This constraint shaped the system as much as any user requirement did, and is the reason WattWise is a plug-in unit controlling two outlets rather than a panel-mounted installation.

The appliance mix in these rooms suits that arrangement. Occupants typically operate mobile telephone and laptop chargers, an electric fan, light-emitting diode lamps, a television or monitor, and a games console — loads well within the capacity of an outlet-level device. Heating and motor-driven appliances, which would exceed it, are generally not present or not permitted.

The tariff is a further reason for choosing this site. As of April 2026 the effective all-in residential rate in the PELCO III area stood at ₱10.2745 per kilowatt-hour, and the cooperative’s statement itemises generation, transmission, distribution, and government charges separately rather than applying a flat rate. Any cost figure meaningful to a household here must reproduce that structure. A system built and validated against PELCO III statements is therefore directly applicable to the locale, and its accuracy is checkable against a document the household already receives each month.

Purpose and Description

Chapter I › between Project Context and Statement of Objectives

This section has to do something the Introduction does not: match each part of the system to a specific difficulty already named. Paste — Chapter I › Purpose and Description

This study proposes WattWise, an IoT-based dual-outlet energy monitoring system with appliance detection, automated safety control, and real-time cost analysis, designed to give the occupant of a rented room the per-appliance visibility the monthly bill withholds.

The system comprises three parts. A plug-in controller — the WattWise Hub — houses an ESP32-WROOM-32 microcontroller, two PZEM-004T energy sensors and a two-channel relay module, and measures and switches two outlets independently. A Firebase cloud backend stores each reading, computes cost against the PELCO III tariff, and runs the scheduled work by which a day of consumption is closed into a permanent record. An Android application and a web dashboard present the same account, so the household may read the same figures on a phone or on a larger screen.

Each element addresses a specific difficulty identified in the locale. Against the absence of per-appliance visibility, the system measures each outlet separately and reports voltage, current, power and accumulated energy for each, so that consumption is attributed to a socket rather than to a room. Against the delay inherent in monthly billing, readings are displayed as they are taken and priced immediately, so that the cost of leaving an appliance running is known while the decision to leave it running can still be reversed. Against the opacity of the statement itself, cost is computed using the PELCO III charge structure rather than a flat rate, and the resulting monthly figure may be compared line by line against the statement the household receives. Against consumption that is discovered only at the end of the month, a monthly budget may be set and the user notified as thresholds are approached. Against the inability to switch off an appliance one is not present to reach, either outlet may be switched remotely, or on a schedule.

Safety is enforced on the controller itself rather than in the cloud. Measured power is compared against a compiled limit of 500 W per outlet and 1,000 W combined, and the affected relay is de-energised without reference to any cloud instruction, so that protection remains in force when the network does not. This matters in a rented room, where the occupant does not control the circuit protection upstream of the socket.

Appliance identification supports this purpose without being relied upon by it. The system matches a measurement run against a catalogue of eight stored profiles and presents the result as a suggestion for the user to confirm; where the leading candidates are too close to separate, it reports that the load cannot be named rather than assigning one. Energy, cost and the safety cut-off are each derived from direct measurement and do not read the inferred name, so the household’s bill and its protection do not depend on the identification being correct.

WattWise does not replace the utility meter and does not measure the whole household. It measures what is plugged into it — two outlets, serving the low-power appliances the locale actually uses — and reports that consumption in pesos, as it happens, to the person who pays for it.

Two tariff figures are in circulation

Chapter I cites ₱10.2745 per kWh as the April 2026 PELCO III rate. The running system is configured at ₱11.03 per kWh, which is what appears on the dashboard and will therefore appear in any Chapter IV screenshot.

Both can be correct — one is a published rate for a stated month, the other is what was entered from a later statement. But a panel comparing your Chapter I to your results will ask. Name the billing period beside the cited figure, and note that rates are entered per billing period from the household’s own statement, which is already how the system works.
